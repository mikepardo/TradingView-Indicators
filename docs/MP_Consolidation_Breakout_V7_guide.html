Consolidation & Breakout Detector v7

A complete field guide to every concept, signal, and setting: how the script finds coils, confirms breakouts, and manages the whole trade — plan, retest, targets, and exits.

§1What it does

The script watches for consolidations — stretches where price coils into an unusually tight range — and treats each one as a setup. When price closes beyond the coil's boundary and holds there, it fires a confirmed BREAK signal and draws a full trade plan: the exact entry level, a stop, and two measured-move targets. From there it keeps working the trade: it flags the retest (price returning to the broken level and holding — the precision entry), warns when a breakout is failing, and marks the stop and both targets as they're hit. Every consolidation also leaves behind a frozen support/resistance zone that stays on the chart, extends forward until decisively broken, and flips roles when it breaks.

Everything confirms on bar close and nothing repaints: zones freeze the moment a break starts, and plan levels never move after they're drawn.

§2The signal lifecycle

Each setup moves through the same pipeline. Understanding it explains every mark the script prints.

Concepts behind the pipeline

  • Prior-bar window. The high–low range is measured over the lookback window ending at the previous bar, so a breakout candle can never distort the very level it is breaking.
  • Expand-only boundaries. While a coil lives, its edges can widen but never shrink. The breakout level is the structural boundary you see — never a decayed artifact of old bars aging out of a rolling window.
  • Freeze at the raw break. The zone's edges, touch counts, and traded levels are captured on the raw-break bar. Everything drawn afterward — box, bands, plan — uses those frozen values.
  • Buffered confirmation. With a breakout buffer set, confirmation closes are held to the same standard as the break itself: a close between the level and level+buffer stalls the count; a close back through the level kills the attempt.
  • One signal per coil. After a confirmed BREAK the coil is spent. A fresh consolidation must form before the next signal can fire.

§3Zone anatomy

Zones are drawn as boxes with shaded bands along their edges, because support and resistance are areas, not lines.

Zone behavior rules

  • Forming (dashed blue border): the live coil preview. It can widen, never shrink.
  • Frozen (solid border): after a breakout (green = up, red = down) or after the coil ends without one (grey). Frozen edges never move.
  • Unbroken zones stay live: a grey zone keeps extending right as active S/R until a close clears an edge by the touch/break band; then it's stamped "▲ broke up" or "▼ broke down".
  • Level basis: with Wicks (default) breaks are tested against wick extremes; with Closes, against body extremes — immune to one-off spike wicks, earlier entries.

§4Two detection engines

What counts as "tight"? The script measures the window's high-to-low range as a percentage of price, then compares it to a threshold. The two modes differ only in where that threshold comes from.

§5The trade plan

On every confirmed BREAK, all plan levels are computed from the frozen zone — they never move afterward.

How the plan resolves

  • STOP ✕ (red): a bar closes through the stop. A wick through it doesn't count; and a bar that wicks a target but closes through the stop counts as stopped — intrabar order is unknowable, so the loss is assumed.
  • T1 ◆: price touches target 1. The plan suggests taking partials / moving the stop to entry (the info table's Plan State says so).
  • T2 ◆: price touches target 2 after T1 — plan complete.
  • FAIL ✕ (orange): independent of the stop — a close back through the broken level means the breakout premise is dead, usually long before the stop is hit. Treat it as the early exit warning.
  • Superseded: a fresh coil forming while a plan is open closes out the old tracking — new structure outranks an old trade.

§6Retest entries

Chasing a confirmation close means paying the worst price of the move so far. The retest signal waits for the market to come back: after a BREAK, if price pulls back to within the Retest Band (an ATR multiple) of the broken level and closes back in the breakout direction, RETEST prints — an entry at nearly the level itself, with the stop naturally close by. Retests are only valid within the Retest Window (bars since the breakout), and each breakout gets at most one.

§10Alerts

Two complementary systems:

  • Alert conditions (pick one when creating an alert): Bullish/Bearish Breakout · Entering Consolidation · Raw Break Up/Down (unconfirmed) · Bullish/Bearish Retest Entry · Failed Breakout · Stop Hit · Target 1 Hit · Target 2 Hit.
  • Dynamic alerts: create one alert on the indicator with "Any alert() function call" and every event arrives with live numbers in the message — e.g. BREAK ▲ HBM 15 | Entry 26.81 | Stop 26.57 | T1 27.27 | T2 27.74.

All alerts fire on closed bars only.

§11Repainting, warm-up & honest limitations

  • Non-repainting by construction. All state changes are gated on confirmed bars: signals, zones and alerts appear only when a bar closes, and confirmed history never redraws. The current unclosed bar can still change — that's the market, not the script.
  • Warm-up. Adaptive mode needs Lookback + Baseline bars of history before it starts flagging.
  • Spike highs are structural. A prior spike stays in the window and can become the resistance the next break must clear. That's deliberate — the touch counts tell you how meaningful the edge is.
  • False breakouts exist. Min Coil Bars, confirmation closes, the ATR buffer and the volume filter reduce them — nothing eliminates them. In relentless chop, demand more confirmation.
  • Educational tool — not financial advice.

Quick triage — "no zones showing?" Open the info table and compare Range % to Threshold. Fixed mode: raise the threshold. Adaptive mode: raise the Compression Factor. Also check that the symbol has enough history for warm-up.
